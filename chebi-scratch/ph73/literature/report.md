# Breaking biochemistry's dependence on Marvin protonation

Nearly every machine-readable "charge at physiological pH" in biochemistry comes from one closed program. Rhea and ChEBI (pH 7.3), MetaCyc (7.3), ModelSEED (7.0), eQuilibrator, MetaNetX and HMDB all run ChemAxon Marvin, and UniProt inherits Rhea's forms ([Bansal 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728268); [Seaver 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7778927); [Beber 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728285)). Agreement between these resources is therefore agreement with Marvin, not independent validation. The open literature supports a clean-room replacement on three grounds. First, at pH 7.3 most metabolite groups sit far from their pKa, so transparent group rules work well. Second, open ML predictors now match commercial accuracy on independent benchmarks ([Sipos-Szabó 2026](https://real.mtak.hu/247419/1/ci6c00107.pdf)). Third, the hard cases (phosphate, sulfite, xanthine, azauracils, tetracycline, quercetin, ATP) are a known short list where any single "major microspecies" is close to arbitrary. The main obstacles are provenance and licensing, not accuracy. Almost all open ML models were pretrained on ChemAxon, Epik or ACD labels. The only large permissively licensed experimental sets are small, drug-like and partly Marvin-filtered. ChemAxon's EULA for licences issued from 1 October 2026 explicitly bans using Output to train, benchmark or build substitutes ([Chemaxon EULA](https://docs.chemaxon.com/latest/legal_end-user-license-agreement-eula.html)). The project's own results show a clean-room rule set does not lose accuracy. The rules in `chemrof.converter.protonation` match 89.5% of measured charges on 2,152 held-out IUPAC molecules, against 89.4% for rules fitted to Rhea, and 95.9% against 94.5% for ChemAxon (via Rhea) on the 217 molecules that both cover, a difference too small to be conclusive (project finding; [benchmark](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23324328)). The recommended course is to keep the rule engine as the default, add explicit borderline flags and a curated metabolite pKa table, and keep anyone holding a ChemAxon licence out of the label-generation and comparison loop.

## One vendor sits behind almost every pH 7.x charge state

Rhea chose pH 7.3 "arbitrarily" in 2012 and uses Marvin to select the major species at 298 K. The same paper concedes that phosphate as HPO4(2−) "is a simplification of the actual state" because H2PO4(−) is nearly as abundant ([Alcántara 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3245052)). The 2015 update states that participants are "verified using the Marvin pKa calculator from ChemAxon (version 6.2.0)" ([Morgat 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4384025)). The Rhea help pages say Marvin computes the pH 7.3 major microspecies "for all ChEBI entities" and that the policy follows MetaCyc ([Rhea help](https://www.rhea-db.org/help/chebi-in-rhea)). Since 2022, Rhea search, including exact search, maps other protonation states onto the pH 7.3 form through a precomputed mapping file ([Bansal 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728268)). ChEBI's pH 7.3 entities carry comments such as "major microspecies at pH 7.3 (according to Marvin v 6.2.0.)" ([CHEBI:90784](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/CHEBI:90784)). Nothing published shows a recomputation with a newer Marvin, so many entries probably still reflect a roughly 2014 engine. UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot imports Rhea cross-references automatically, so enzyme annotations inherit these forms ([Morgat 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4384025)).

Analysis of Rhea release 142's `chebi_pH7_3_mapping.tsv` (2026-09-02) explains how the mapping was made. It has 122,732 rows. All 8,900 rows that change the form are labelled `computation`, and all 898 `curation` rows map an entity to itself (project finding; [mapping file](https://ftp.expasy.org/databases/rhea/tsv/chebi_pH7_3_mapping.tsv)). No Rhea document defines the ORIGIN column. The pattern suggests curators assert "use this entity as-is", often for generic or polymeric entities Marvin cannot handle, and do not substitute alternative charge states. Every non-trivial pH 7.3 conversion in Rhea is therefore Marvin output.

The metabolic-modelling stack follows the same pattern with different reference pHs. MetaCyc adjusted all compounds to pH 7.3 from version 13.0 onward using Marvin. When proto-isomers are close in abundance it picks the higher floating-point value ([PGDB Concepts Guide](https://metacyc.org/PGDBConceptsGuide.shtml)). An SRI curator reported Marvin splitting ATP about 57% (3−) to 43% (4−) and choosing ATP(3−) ([ChemAxon forum](https://forum.chemaxon.com/6/4114/problems-with-pka-plugin-accuracy/)), while Rhea now maps ATP to ATP(4−) ([mapping file](https://ftp.expasy.org/databases/rhea/tsv/chebi_pH7_3_mapping.tsv)). ModelSEED protonates at pH 7.0 with Marvin and falls back to RDKit/Open Babel when Marvin fails ([Seaver 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7778927)). In September 2026 it regenerated its bundles with Marvin 26.1, which changed net charge for 20.3% of shared compounds. It could not use tautomer handling because that requires a separate "Isomers Plugin Group" licence ([ModelSEEDDatabase PR #295](https://github.com/ModelSEED/ModelSEEDDatabase/pull/295)). This 20% figure is the clearest published measure of how unstable vendor-derived charges are across versions. eQuilibrator computes pKa ensembles with Marvin 21.13 under an academic licence and requires `cxcalc` on the PATH to build a local cache ([Beber 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728285); [equilibrator-assets](https://pypi.org/project/equilibrator-assets)). MetaNetX lists MarvinBeans in its pipeline. It deliberately merges protonation states, so cross-resource charge disagreements disappear and "acid-base reactions become empty" ([Moretti 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7778905/); [MetaNetX RDF schema](https://rdf.metanetx.org/schema/CPLX)). Recon 2 fixed charges at pH 7.2 in every compartment ([Thiele 2013](https://www.nature.com/articles/nbt.2488)). BiGG stores a formula plus an integer charge and has not been updated since 2019 ([BiGG 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7145653)). HMDB shows ChemAxon-predicted "Physiological Charge" and strongest acidic and basic pKa values ([HMDB0040256](https://hmdb.ca/metabolites/HMDB0040256)). The notes found no primary documentation for KEGG, LIPID MAPS or Reactome, and no confirmed tool for Recon3D/VMH, so claims about those resources remain unverified.

| Resource | Reference pH | Engine (documented) | Curator override |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rhea / ChEBI / UniProt | 7.3 | Marvin 6.2.0 (2015 statement) | 898 self-mapping "curation" rows |
| MetaCyc | 7.3 | Marvin | Proton-only rebalancing |
| ModelSEED | 7.0 | Marvin 19.1, then 26.1 (2026) | RDKit/Open Babel fallback |
| eQuilibrator | ensemble | Marvin 21.13 | Manual Alberty overrides |
| MetaNetX | merged | MarvinBeans 19.26 | Ignores protonation in IDs |
| Recon 2 | 7.2 | Not stated | LP charge balancing (Recon 2.2) |
| HMDB | ~7.4 (unconfirmed) | ChemAxon | None documented |

No resource has replaced ChemAxon in production. eQuilibrator's lead developer has called the proprietary pKa dependency "the problem" since 2020 ([equilibrator-users](https://groups.google.com/g/equilibrator-users/c/BXvntaUswIg)). A 2024 BioHackathon proposal from SIB (MetaNetX/Rhea) described Marvin's validation as "opaque", said ChemAxon's academic licence had become "much more restrictive", and called for open alternatives such as Dimorphite-DL and pkasolver ([HackMD proposal](https://hackmd.io/@smoretti/SJ-bYnPx0T)). No outcome from that proposal was found.

## Open predictors match commercial accuracy but inherit commercial labels

The largest independent benchmark, pKaHub, has more than 90,000 experimental values on more than 31,000 molecules. Its test sets do not overlap any predictor's training data. It concludes that open tools, "especially Uni-pKa, have caught up with commercial products". Commercial tools still fail on fewer than 1% of inputs, against 6.6% for Uni-pKa and 7.4% for pkasolver. Macro-pKa predictors beat micro-pKa predictors on polyprotic molecules ([Sipos-Szabó 2026](https://real.mtak.hu/247419/1/ci6c00107.pdf)). On the older small sets, pkasolver reports Novartis/Literature RMSE of 0.93/0.82 against Marvin's 1.17/0.87 ([Mayr 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fchem.2022.866585/full)). QupKake reports 0.79/0.54, and 0.44 on SAMPL6 against 0.61 for Epik 7 ([Abarbanel & Hutchison 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11325546/)). Graph-pKa reaches SAMPL6 RMSE 0.73 but trails ChemAxon on SAMPL7 (0.93 vs 0.71) ([Xiong 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8756178)).

Provenance is the problem for a clean-room project. pKaHub notes that "almost every recently published method" pretrains on large sets of calculated pKa values from ChEMBL ([Sipos-Szabó 2026](https://real.mtak.hu/247419/1/ci6c00107.pdf)). The source program differs by model. MolGpKa used ACD/Labs values. QupKake used ChEMBL v32 ChemAxon values, about 2.5 M of them, and the benchmark notes it "significantly outperforms" ChemAxon partly because of that pretraining. pkasolver pretrained on Epik scans, and its authors say they "are unable to distribute models trained using results generated with Epik". Uni-pKa and MF-SuP-pKa pretrain on "predicted" ChEMBL pKa values from an unnamed tool ([Mayr 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fchem.2022.866585/full); [Uni-pKa](https://github.com/dptech-corp/Uni-pKa); [MF-SuP-pKa](https://github.com/wujialu/MF-SuP-pKa)). ChEMBL has supplied ChemAxon pKa columns since release 26 and ACD values before that ([ChEMBL 26 notes](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/chembl/ChEMBLdb/releases/chembl_26/chembl_26_release_notes.txt)), so "ChEMBL pKa" pretraining almost always means vendor labels. The fine-tuning data is usually the Baltruschat & Czodrowski set, and it is not vendor-free either. Marvin removed multiprotic molecules and outliers more than 4 units from its own prediction, and OpenEye QUACPAC set protonation at pH 7.4 ([Baltruschat & Czodrowski 2020](https://f1000research.com/articles/9-113/v2)). Graph-pKa is the one strong model that reports only experimental labels, but its code licence is unclear. The distributable "pkasolver-light" weights were fine-tuned only on the Marvin-filtered set.

The tools also differ in what they output. Only Dimorphite-DL (Apache-2.0) returns protonated structures at a target pH. It returns every variant whose pKa window overlaps a pH range (6.4–8.4 by default), not a single major species. It also has documented gaps: weak aromatic N bases appear in both states, acylguanidines appear only as cations, and nucleobase and tetrazole coverage is narrow ([Dimorphite-DL](https://github.com/durrantlab/dimorphite_dl)). Its per-group pKa means were derived from 1,938 single-site molecules, mostly with experimental values ([Ropp 2019](https://www.doi.org/10.1186/s13321-019-0336-9)). The ML tools return micro- or macro-pKa values. Uni-pKa enumerates microstates and predicts their free energies, which makes a Boltzmann argmax at pH 7.3 straightforward but not a built-in function. No tool paper reports accuracy on phosphates, nucleotides or other polyprotic metabolites. The training sets are filtered toward drug-likeness, with Lipinski filters, monoprotic molecules only and a pKa range of 2–12 ([Baltruschat & Czodrowski 2020](https://f1000research.com/articles/9-113/v2)), so performance on Rhea's chemical space is unknown.

| Tool | Code licence | Label provenance | Major species at pH? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dimorphite-DL | Apache-2.0 | Mostly experimental, single-site | Enumerates a window |
| pkasolver | MIT | Epik pretraining; Marvin-filtered fine-tuning | Sequential micro-pKa |
| MolGpKa | MIT | ACD/Labs on ChEMBL | Micro-pKa |
| QupKake | Not confirmed | ChemAxon on ChEMBL v32 | Micro-pKa per site |
| Uni-pKa | Apache-2.0 (weights not in repo) | Unnamed predicted ChEMBL values | Microstate free energies |
| Graph-pKa | Unclear | Experimental (17,489 values) | Macro, deconvoluted |
| Open Babel phmodel | GPL-2 | One fixed pKa per SMARTS | Single structure, no interactions |

## Permissive experimental pKa data is scarce and drug-biased

The IUPAC Digitized Dissociation Constants set is the broadest open source of measured values. It digitizes Perrin (bases, 1965/1972) and Serjeant & Dempsey (acids, 1979) into 24,211 rows covering 10,626 molecules, with temperature, method, reliability and site remarks. It is licensed **CC BY-NC 4.0** and requires the attribution "Reproduced by permission of International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry" ([IUPAC/Dissociation-Constants](https://github.com/IUPAC/Dissociation-Constants)). Its structures came from OPSIN, PubChem and CIR, with help from ChemAxon Molconvert. Site labels follow a heuristic: with two values, the lower is called basic and the higher acidic. It is excellent for testing but cannot go into a CC0 or CC BY artifact.

The Baltruschat & Czodrowski extracts (8,111 exact ChEMBL25 values and 7,911 DataWarrior entries) are the main permissive alternative. The repository's datasets/LICENSE.txt is CC BY 4.0, which the project verified directly (project finding). The F1000 article says MIT ([Baltruschat & Czodrowski 2020](https://f1000research.com/articles/9-113/v2); [GitHub](https://github.com/czodrowskilab/Machine-learning-meets-pKa)). Two caveats remain. The ChEMBL portion originates from a CC BY-SA 3.0 database ([ChEMBL LICENSE](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/chembl/ChEMBLdb/latest/LICENSE)). The DataWarrior file was "given to me about 20 years ago", and its literature references "got lost or [were] never collected", although its protonation sites were curated by hand ([openmolecules forum](https://openmolecules.org/forum/pdf.php?th=96)). The project's decision to use only the raw extracts, not the authors' Marvin-filtered and Marvin-labelled combined files, is the correct reading of this provenance. It removes the ChemAxon step at the cost of re-doing site assignment.

The other sources each fail on licence or scope. pKaHub (more than 90,000 values with microspecies distributions) is the most promising new source, but only the article's licence (CC BY 4.0) is confirmed. Its data terms and how its microspecies annotations were made are not, and Epik was used to build the database ([Sipos-Szabó 2026](https://real.mtak.hu/247419/1/ci6c00107.pdf); [pKaHub](https://github.com/keserulab/pkahub)). iBonD is "free of charge for non-profit academic use" and mostly non-aqueous ([SJTU](https://news.sjtu.edu.cn/xsjz/20201018/132513.html)). NIST SRD 46 (Martell & Smith, 6,166 ligands) is the classic source for nucleotides and phosphates, but it is discontinued and "All rights reserved" ([NIST SRD 46](https://www.nist.gov/srd/nist46)). SAMPL6 is the only set found with NMR-confirmed microstates, and it has just 24 drug-like fragments ([Işık 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/368787v2)). No open, NMR-derived micro-pKa compilation for metabolites exists. Experimentally determined site labels for amino acid zwitterions, nucleotide phosphate versus adenine N1, and purine N3/N7/N9 must be hand-curated from primary papers. Individual pKa values are facts. The restrictions attach to compilations, and the EU sui generis right lasts 15 years ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/legal-protection-databases.html)). Re-extracting a few hundred metabolite values from primary literature with per-value citations is therefore the clean path to a CC0 or CC BY metabolite table.

## Borderline biochemicals cluster within half a unit of pH 7.3

Henderson–Hasselbalch sets the limits. When a macro-pKa lies within 0.5 units of 7.3, the minor form is at least 24% of the total, and typical predictor errors of 0.5–1 unit can flip the call. ChemAxon's own validation summary reports errors of 0.1 ± 1.1 for single-site compounds and 0.9 ± 3.2 for amphoteric ones ([ChemAxon validation](https://dl.chemaxon.com/docs/HTML/docs1750/Validation_results.html)). A 2024 preprint reports microstate errors near 2 units for EDTA and amino acids ([chemRxiv 2024](https://chemrxiv.org/doi/pdf/10.26434/chemrxiv-2024-msd0q-v2)). Developers working with ChemAxon output have hand-patched spurious pKa values for metal cations ([dGPredictor compound.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/vuu10/dGPredictor/blob/main/CC/compound.py)). Marvin 6.1.3–6.1.4 could not compute the microspecies of water, a bug found during Rhea normalization ([ChemAxon forum](https://forum.chemaxon.com/6/9347/major-microspecies-plugin-does-not-work/)). No published, systematic audit of ChEBI's pH 7.3 forms against measured values exists.

**Phosphates and phosphonates.** Inorganic phosphate (pKa2 7.21) is about 55:45 HPO4(2−):H2PO4(−) at pH 7.3 ([Saylor appendix](https://saylordotorg.github.io/text_general-chemistry-principles-patterns-and-applications-v1.0/s31-appendix-c-dissociation-consta.html)). Sulfite (pKa2 7.2) behaves the same way ([LibreTexts E1](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Ancillary_Materials/Reference/Reference_Tables/Equilibrium_Constants/E1%3A_Acid_Dissociation_Constants_at_25C)). ATP's terminal pK is 7.60 at zero ionic strength ([Gnaiger 1994](https://mitofit.org/images/a/a4/Gnaiger_1994_BTK-207.pdf)), but considerably lower at physiological ionic strength and with Mg2+, which is why eQuilibrator corrects for Mg2+ explicitly ([Beber 2022](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/50/D1/D603/6445959); [Alberty 1993](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1225844)). ATP(4−) is therefore defensible only under an ionic-strength-corrected convention, and the MetaCyc and Rhea calls for ATP have differed. Glyphosate's values vary between compilations, but every set gives a net −2 at pH 7.3 ([ATSDR](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/ToxProfiles/tp214-c4.pdf)). This illustrates that the decision often holds even when the numbers are uncertain.

**Purines, pyrimidines and azauracils.** Urate (pKa1 5.4) is a monoanion ([chemistry-online](https://www.chemistry-online.com/molecules/uric-acid/)). Whether N3 or N9 loses the proton is disputed: DFT favours N3, while a 2023 study finds two comparable monoanions ([Int J Quantum Chem 2004](https://onlinelibrary-wiley-com-443.webvpn.zafu.edu.cn/doi/abs/10.1002/qua.20246); [Cumhuriyet 2023](https://acikerisim.cumhuriyet.edu.tr/items/aea07318-6dff-4ab4-9b69-da92d0ce9262)). This changes the InChI but not the charge. Free xanthine is nearly evenly split between neutral and anionic forms at physiological pH ([UTMB](https://researchexperts.utmb.edu/en/publications/first-principles-calculations-of-the-pksubasub-values-and-tautome/)). Xanthosine and its nucleotides ionize at N3 with pKa about 5.3–5.7 and are clearly anionic ([Acta Biochim Pol](https://abp.ptbioch.edu.pl/index.php/abp/article/download/3587/2645)), while guanine and hypoxanthine lose N1-H only at higher pH. 6-Azauridine's N3-H pKa is 6.8, against 9.3 for uridine, so it is about 76% anionic ([PMC3466602](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3466602); [PMC7726947](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7726947)). A rule that treats it as "a uridine" gets the charge wrong. 1-Methyladenosine (pKa about 8.25) is about 90% cationic but not permanently charged ([PMC101250](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC101250)).

**Flavins, tetracyclines and flavonoids.** Riboflavin's ring pKa is 9.69, so the ring is neutral at pH 7.3 ([Sigma-Aldrich R9504](https://perfqws.sigmaaldrich.com/deepweb/assets/sigmaaldrich/product/documents/191/415/r9504pis.pdf)) and the charges of FMN and FAD come from their phosphates. A literature N1 pKa near 6.7 for reduced flavins would make FMNH2 and FADH2 borderline, but this was not verified. Tetracycline (pKa 3.3, 7.7, 9.7) is about 72% zwitterion and 28% anion at pH 7.3 ([Sigma-Aldrich T3383](https://perfqws.sigmaaldrich.com/deepweb/assets/sigmaaldrich/product/documents/348/160/t3383pis.pdf)). ChEBI's choice of the zwitterion "according to Marvin" ([CHEBI:77932](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/CHEBI:77932)) matches the macro-pKa, but which site the anion loses (C10 or C12) is a microspecies question. Quercetin's first pKa of 7.03 makes the monoanion about 65% ([Z Naturforsch B 1998](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/znb-1998-0310/html)), so a neutral pH 7.3 form is probably wrong. Predicted flavonoid ionization varies widely between methods ([Simulations Plus 2023](https://www.simulations-plus.com/wp-content/uploads/Ioniz-Flavonoids-14Nov2023.pdf)).

**Polyamines and imino acids.** Literature pK values for spermine (11.50, 10.95, 9.79, 8.90) give about 97% as the +4 form, and spermidine and putrescine are fully protonated ([Molecules 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6100306/table/molecules-23-01112-t001)). Older reports of the last spermine step vary by about one unit, and NMR work shows microscopic coupling between the amines ([Anal Bioanal Chem 2003](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00216-003-2020-0)). 1-Pyrroline-5-carboxylate has reported pKa values of 1.82 and 6.07 ([Wikipedia: P5C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-Pyrroline-5-carboxylic_acid)). If 6.07 belongs to the imine, the −1 anion with a neutral C=N dominates (about 94%), not a proline-like iminium zwitterion. The primary measurement has not been traced, and P5C's ring–chain equilibrium with glutamate semialdehyde lies outside any microspecies model ([HMDB0001301](https://www.hmdb.ca/metabolites/HMDB0001301)). Histidine's side chain (6.0) is mostly neutral, and cysteine (8.39) is mostly protonated ([PMC2286538](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2286538/table/t3)).

**Micro- versus macro-pKa.** Potentiometric and UV titrations measure macroscopic constants. Assigning them to sites needs NMR or other evidence ([Wilson 2023](https://www.mpinat.mpg.de/4917080/Wilson_2023_chemRxiv.pdf)). In SAMPL7, participants matched macro-pKa values reasonably well but disagreed on the microscopic transitions ([SAMPL7 evaluation](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10822-021-00397-3)). Net charge, which the project benchmark scores, is a macro property. The specific ChEBI entity, which the Rhea mapping assigns, is a micro property. Tautomer choices such as urate N3 versus N9 change identifiers but pass every charge-balance check. The project's input-side finding adds to this: 12,689 ChEBI SMILES omit the H on aromatic N-H (project finding; [chemkg/chemrof#101](https://github.com/chemkg/chemrof/issues/101)). This hits exactly the purine and imidazole classes where site identity matters, so a predictor fed these SMILES can mislocate or miscount ionizable protons before any pKa logic runs.

## Group-increment rules are competitive where their coverage holds

Classical linear free-energy methods are accurate inside the classes they parameterize. Clark & Perrin reported about ±0.07 units on 25 benzoic acids and ±0.28 on 11 anilines, as quoted in the Perrin monograph ([Perrin excerpt](https://www.scribd.com/document/521452239/PK-a-Prediction-for-Organic-Acids-and-Bases)). The monograph organizes Hammett and Taft equations (pKa = pKa0 − ρΣσ) class by class ([Springer](https://www.springer.com/gp/book/9789400958852)). On drug-like sets the mature engines built on these ideas do as well as modern GNNs. ACD/pKa DB reached MAE 0.478 and SPARC 0.651 on 197 pharmaceuticals ([Liao & Nicklaus 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7289148)), against RMSE 0.72 (median absolute error 0.42) for Epik 7 ([Epik 7 preprint](https://chemrxiv.org/engage/chemrxiv/article-details/64188769dab08ad68f6bc895)). A naive lookup of one pKa per group does much worse. Sayle's atom-type table reached only R² 0.80 on 22 drugs, with errors up to 4.5 units, against R² 0.978 for ACD ([Sayle, Daylight 2000](https://www.daylight.com/meetings/emug00/Sayle/pkapredict.html)). The quality comes from the substituent increments and from treating charged neighbours properly. Empirical methods are weakest for amines and for large flexible molecules ([Lee & Crippen 2009](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26762524_Predicting_pK)).

For polyprotic molecules there are two architectures. The greedy approach, used by Sayle/Daylight and Epik Classic, starts from the neutral form, ionizes the most acidic or most basic site, recomputes the remaining pKa values with the new charge treated as a substituent, and repeats until stable. Starting from neutral lets it find zwitterions ([Sayle](https://www.daylight.com/meetings/emug00/Sayle/pkapredict.html); [Shelley 2007](https://www.proquest.com/docview/737270775)). The microconstant approach, used by ChemAxon and SPARC, predicts every site's pKa in every microstate and sums the partition function ([ChemAxon methods PDF](https://dl.chemaxon.com/docs/HTML/docs1750/attachments_49191983_1_Calculating_pKa_values_of_small_and_large_molecules.pdf); [EPA SPARC report](https://archive.epa.gov/athens/archive-athens/web/pdf/epa_600_r03_030.pdf)). The formal basis is Ullmann's treatment of binding polynomials ([cited in Wilson 2023](https://www.mpinat.mpg.de/4917080/Wilson_2023_chemRxiv.pdf)). Microstate free energies follow from path-consistent micro-pKa values, Boltzmann weights at pH 7.3 give the populations, and the major microspecies is the argmax. This framework also yields a population margin for free, which is what a borderline flag needs. Open Babel's phmodel applies fixed-pKa SMARTS transforms in file order with no interactions between sites, and users report it is unreliable ([Open Babel OBPhModel](https://openbabel.org/api/3.0/classOpenBabel_1_1OBPhModel.shtml); [KNIME forum](https://forum.knime.com/t/molecule-protonation-state/3491)).

No maintained open implementation of full Perrin/Hammett–Taft prediction was found in RDKit, CDK or elsewhere. Sayle's description of more than 170 class equations is the most detailed public blueprint, and no code was released. The canonical σ tables (Hansch, Leo & Taft, *Chem. Rev.* 1991) are copyrighted compilations with no open data licence, although the individual constants circulate freely ([Zipse handout](https://zipse.cup.uni-muenchen.de/site/assets/files/1480/hammett_19_06_02.pdf)). A clean-room engine can avoid them. It can derive σ from open benzoic acid and phenol pKa values, or fit increments directly to permissively licensed data, as the project has done. The project's rules fitted only to raw Baltruschat extracts plus textbook group pKa values match the accuracy of rules fitted to Rhea (89.5% vs 89.4% on 2,152 IUPAC molecules) (project finding). Fitting to vendor output therefore buys no accuracy on measured data. The 90.2% agreement with Rhea's changed rows (91.6% for Rhea-fitted rules) shows the 1.4-point gain from fitting to Rhea is agreement with Marvin, not with experiment.

## Contract, not copyright, governs ChemAxon outputs

ChemAxon's EULA for licences issued on or after 1 October 2026 is the most restrictive text in this space. Output may be used "solely for its internal business purposes" (§2.5). §2.6(b) prohibits using Software or Output "to pre-train, train, fine-tune … or create a training dataset", and using Output "as an evaluation or benchmarking corpus to develop, improve, or commercialize an AI System or other product or service". §2.6(c) bars using Output "to reconstruct, reproduce, or create a substitute for the Chemaxon Software" or to build a competing product. §3.2(f) and (k) prohibit publishing benchmarking results or "comparisons of data generated by using Chemaxon Software" without written consent. The governing law is Hungarian, and ChemAxon may amend the terms on 30 days' notice. §2.7 states that ChemAxon claims no ownership of Output ([Chemaxon EULA](https://docs.chemaxon.com/latest/legal_end-user-license-agreement-eula.html)). The January 2023 EULA, which covers licences issued up to 30 September 2026, contains the reverse-engineering and publish-comparison bans and the internal-use limit, but no clause on output use, AI or competition ([Chemaxon EULA until 2026-09-30](https://docs.chemaxon.com/latest/legal_end-user-license-agreement-eula-until-2026-09-30.html)). That older text defines "User" broadly enough to include anyone who "uses the results generated through Chemaxon Software in another interactive software". This is a scope definition for licence counting, not a grant of rights against non-licensees.

Contracts bind licensees, and computed values are weak candidates for intellectual property. In the US, *Feist* holds that facts are not copyrightable ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340)), and *Thaler v. Perlmutter* requires human authorship ([IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/03/24/d-c-circuit-upholds-human-authorship-requirement-thaler-v-perlmutter/)). In the EU, investment in *creating* data does not count toward sui generis database protection ([BHB v William Hill, CURIA](https://curia.europa.eu/en/actu/communiques/cp04/aff/cp040089en.pdf)). Any database right in Rhea's mapping would belong to SIB, which publishes Rhea under CC BY 4.0 for commercial and non-commercial reuse ([Rhea licence](https://www.rhea-db.org/help/license-disclaimer)). *SAS v World Programming* holds that functionality is not protected expression, and that a licensee's right to "observe, study or test" software cannot be overridden by contract ([Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-limited)). *Ryanair v PR Aviation*, however, lets owners of unprotected databases impose contractual limits ([SCL](https://www.scl.org/3280-the-cjeu-takes-flight-databases-and-air-fares/)). Community practice matches this reading. ChEMBL redistributes ChemAxon pKa values under CC BY-SA 3.0, and Rhea redistributes Marvin microspecies under CC BY 4.0. The one documented case of restraint, pkasolver withholding Epik-trained weights, came from the trainers' own Epik licence ([pkasolver README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mayrf/pkasolver/main/README.md)).

The practical consequence is a clear line, offered as analysis and not legal advice. A team member who downloads Rhea's CC BY file and never accepts a ChemAxon EULA is not a party to the contract. Comparing against that file creates little legal exposure, though it is not zero: unfair-competition or trade-secret theories are untested. The exposure rises sharply if anyone on the project holds a ChemAxon licence, particularly one issued after 1 October 2026, and runs `cxcalc` to generate labels, comparisons or "agreement with Marvin" figures. §2.6(b)–(c) and §3.2(f),(k) then apply directly. Separately, CC licence obligations attach only to copyrightable or sui generis content. Abstracted SMARTS rules with group pKa values are very likely facts or ideas and can be released as CC0. Bundled tables of IUPAC values must stay CC BY-NC with IUPAC's attribution, and substantial ChEMBL extracts must stay CC BY-SA ([CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/); [IUPAC README](https://github.com/IUPAC/Dissociation-Constants)).

## Concrete recommendations for the clean-room predictor

The literature and the project's results point to a predictor that is rule-based at its core, thermodynamically explicit about multi-site coupling, and honest about borderline cases. The table below collects the actions. Each one addresses a gap the evidence above identifies.

| Area | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Provenance | Keep the clean-room rules as the default. Use the Rhea-fitted variant only as a diagnostic, never in released rules. | It matches measured data equally well (89.5% vs 89.4%); its extra Rhea agreement is agreement with Marvin. |
| Personnel | Document that no one generating labels or publishing comparisons holds an active ChemAxon licence. Never run `cxcalc` in the pipeline. | Post-1 Oct 2026 EULA §2.6(b)–(c) and §3.2(f),(k) bind licensees. |
| Evaluation hierarchy | Score primarily against measured pKa (IUPAC, SAMPL6, curated metabolite set). Report agreement with Rhea as secondary, labelled "agreement with Marvin 6.2 output". | Agreement with Rhea, MetaCyc, ModelSEED or eQuilibrator is agreement with one engine. |
| Engine | Move from greedy per-group decisions to microstate enumeration with Boltzmann weights at pH 7.3. Model charged neighbours as substituents (Taft σ* with distance attenuation) and include statistical factors. | Microconstant and iterative schemes are what lift rule engines from R² 0.80 to 0.98 territory. |
| Borderline flag | Emit population margins. Flag any entity with p_max < 0.8 or any site with \|pKa − 7.3\| < 0.5–1.0, and record both forms. | Phosphate, sulfite, xanthine, azauracils, tetracycline, quercetin and ATP sit in this window. |
| Biochemical rule coverage | Add explicit rules for phosphate monoesters and diesters, pyrophosphates, phosphonates, sulfates, N3-H of azauracils, xanthine N3, N1-alkylated adenines, cyclic imines (P5C type), flavonoid 7-OH, and tetracycline enol systems. | These are the documented error classes, and Dimorphite-DL and phmodel cover them poorly. |
| Conventions | State ionic strength and Mg2+ assumptions for nucleotides. Choose an explicit convention for ATP and Pi and document it. | ATP's terminal pK moves from 7.6 (I = 0) to well below that at physiological I. |
| Input hygiene | Repair or flag the 12,689 ChEBI SMILES with missing aromatic N-H before prediction (chemkg/chemrof#101). Report tautomer-only disagreements separately from charge disagreements. | Site identity matters most in purines and imidazoles, and tautomer errors pass charge checks. |
| Data | Build a CC0 or CC BY metabolite pKa table of about 100–300 hand-curated values from primary literature, cited per value. Use IUPAC, NIST SRD 46 and iBonD only as cross-checks. | No open, permissive metabolite pKa set exists. Compilations carry NC, SA or all-rights-reserved terms. |
| Licensing of artifacts | Release rules and code as CC0 or permissive. Keep IUPAC values and Baltruschat-derived ChEMBL rows out of redistributed data, or carry CC BY-NC (with IUPAC's attribution) or CC BY-SA respectively if they are ever included. The published benchmark (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23324328) already follows this: it contains only ChEBI- and Rhea-derived data and predictions, and no IUPAC or measured-pKa values (project finding, per its data card). | NC and SA terms attach to substantial extracts, not to abstracted rules. |
| Optional ML layer | If an ML tie-breaker is wanted for borderline cases, prefer a model trained only on experimental data (Graph-pKa style), or retrain Uni-pKa's free-energy architecture on clean data. Do not ship vendor-pretrained weights as the default. | Nearly all distributed open weights carry ChemAxon, Epik or ACD pretraining. |
| Community | Share the benchmark and borderline list with the SIB/MetaNetX pKa-benchmarking effort and with ModelSEED. | Both have stated a need for open replacements. |

## Conclusion

The literature changes what the problem looks like. The difficult part of open pH 7.3 protonation was never average accuracy: for a metabolite whose groups sit two or more units from 7.3, Perrin-style rules, Marvin and modern GNNs all agree, and the project shows that clean-room rules equal or slightly exceed ChemAxon-via-Rhea against measured values. The real problem is that the field has used one engine's argmax as ground truth for a decade, including for species where the honest answer is "about 55:45". That convention hides two defects: ModelSEED's 20% net-charge shift between Marvin versions, and a small set of chemically wrong calls such as neutral quercetin or iminium P5C. Neither shows up while every resource uses the same engine.

The open predictor can therefore do better than Rhea in a specific sense. It can expose populations and flag borderline entities, separate tautomer from charge disagreements, and trace every rule to permissively licensed experimental data. The tightening ChemAxon EULA makes this urgent. Licensees can no longer use Marvin output to benchmark or train substitutes, so open, measured-data benchmarks such as the project's Zenodo release become the only defensible yardstick. Rhea, MetaCyc and ModelSEED could plausibly adopt such a tool in place of vendor output, and the strongest argument for doing so is reproducibility, not accuracy.
