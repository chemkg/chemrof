# Legal and licensing considerations: using ChemAxon-computed outputs (Rhea chebi_pH7_3_mapping.tsv) to build an open protonation tool

Background notes for a team discussion. This is not legal advice. Verbatim clause text was extracted directly from the live Chemaxon pages on 2026-10-14. The analysis ("Inferences") is the researcher's reading and has not been reviewed by a lawyer.

## Q1. Do ChemAxon's published licence terms restrict use of calculation results, reverse engineering, benchmarking or competing products?

### Takeaway
Yes. The current EULA (published 30 Sept 2026, for licences issued on or after 1 Oct 2026) adds explicit bans on using "Output" for model training, as a benchmarking corpus, or to "create a substitute for" or compete with Chemaxon software. It also limits Output to "internal business purposes". The earlier EULA (Jan 2023, for licences issued up to 30 Sept 2026) has no output-use or anti-competition clause. Both versions ban reverse engineering and publishing benchmark or comparison results without consent. These are contract terms, so they bind the licensee (Rhea/SIB, or whoever ran Marvin). They do not automatically bind third parties who only download Rhea's CC BY 4.0 file.

### Inferences
- The Rhea pH 7.3 mapping was almost certainly produced under licences that predate 1 Oct 2026, so the Jan 2023 EULA or an earlier one (or a bespoke SIB–Chemaxon agreement) would govern SIB. Those versions contain no "no competing product" or "no ML training" clause on outputs. They do limit use to "internal business purposes" and bar publishing "comparisons of data generated by using Chemaxon Software". Rhea has published the outputs openly for years, which suggests SIB has (or believes it has) permission to redistribute. I found no public SIB–Chemaxon agreement.
- Privity: EULA obligations bind the "Licensee" (and, under the new §2.6(b), the Licensee must not "permit" third parties to train). A downstream user who only downloads Rhea's TSV and never installs Chemaxon software is not a party to the EULA. Any exposure for that user would have to come from IP rights in the data (see Q2), not from contract. The new §2.6(b) "shall not permit ... other third party" wording does create risk for licensees who publish Output from 1 Oct 2026 onward, but that risk falls on the publisher.
- Risk is higher if team members themselves hold a Chemaxon licence (academic or commercial) and run cxcalc to generate fresh labels, comparisons or benchmarks for the open tool. From 1 Oct 2026, §2.6(b)–(c) and §3.2(f),(k) would apply directly to them.
- §3.2(k) ("publish the result of comparisons of data generated by using Chemaxon Software") is broad. A licensee publishing "our tool agrees with Marvin on X% of ChEBI" could fall within it. A non-licensee comparing against Rhea's public file is outside the contract.
- EU mandatory law may override parts of the EULA for licensees: Software Directive Art. 5(3) and Art. 8 (observe/study/test; see Q2). A contractual ban on using outputs (as opposed to studying the program) is not obviously caught by that override.

### Gaps
- I could not find the exact wording of the 2021 "YOU MAY NOT" disclosure clause, or any EULA that governed Rhea's Marvin 6.2 use (c. 2012).
- No public information on whether SIB/Rhea or EMBL-EBI hold bespoke agreements with Chemaxon that permit redistribution.
- I found no published current terms for a Chemaxon free academic licence after the 2023 deletion. Whether academic licences issued after 1 Oct 2026 use the new EULA is not stated.
- I could not determine whether the 30-day modification clause (§12.6 new / equivalent old) lets Chemaxon apply the new AI and anti-competition terms to existing licensees on renewal.

## Q2. Are computed chemical property values protectable by copyright or database rights (US, EU)? How does a CC BY 4.0 release by a licensee interact with vendor terms?

### Takeaway
Individual computed values (a pKa number, a "major microspecies = CHEBI:57416" assertion) are facts or machine output and are very unlikely to be copyrightable in the US or EU. A database as a whole may attract EU sui generis protection, but only for investment in obtaining, verifying or presenting data, not in creating it (BHB v William Hill). Any such right in Rhea's file belongs to SIB, not Chemaxon, and SIB has licensed it under CC BY 4.0. Chemaxon's practical leverage is contractual, and contracts bind only licensees. The new EULA §2.7 expressly disclaims any Chemaxon ownership of Output.

### Cited Findings
- US: *Feist v. Rural Telephone*, 499 U.S. 340 (1991). "Since facts do not owe their origin to an act of authorship, they are not original and, thus, are not copyrightable". The Court rejected "sweat of the brow", and compilations are protected only for original selection or arrangement. — [Cornell LII, 499 U.S. 340](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340)
- US: *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, 130 F.4th 1039 (D.C. Cir. Mar. 18, 2025) held that the Copyright Act requires human authorship, so purely machine-generated output is not registrable. One source reports that cert was denied on March 2, 2026; this is single-sourced and should be verified. — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/03/24/d-c-circuit-upholds-human-authorship-requirement-thaler-v-perlmutter/); [Emory Lawyer 2026](https://law.emory.edu/lawyer/issues/2026/summer/features/ai_copyright/index.html)
- EU sui generis: in *British Horseracing Board v William Hill* (C-203/02, 9 Nov 2004), "substantial investment" "covers only the work involved in seeking, collecting, verifying and presenting existing materials and not the resources used to create the materials which make up the database." — [CURIA press release 89/04](https://curia.europa.eu/en/actu/communiques/cp04/aff/cp040089en.pdf); [Wikipedia: Database Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database_Directive)
- EU contract override: in *Ryanair v PR Aviation* (C-30/14, 15 Jan 2015), the Database Directive "is not applicable to a database which is not protected either by copyright or by the sui generis right". The owner of an unprotected database can therefore impose contractual limits, and the Directive's mandatory user rights (Arts 6(1), 8, 15) do not apply. — [SCL commentary](https://www.scl.org/3280-the-cjeu-takes-flight-databases-and-air-fares/); [Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/ryanair-ltd-v-pr-aviation-bv-contracts-rights-and-users-in-a-low-cost-database-law/)
- EU software: in *SAS Institute v World Programming* (C-406/10, 2 May 2012), "neither the functionality of a computer program nor the programming language and the format of data files ... constitute a form of expression of that program". A licensee may "observe, study or test the functioning of that software in order to determine the ideas and principles which underlie any element of the program. Any contractual provisions contrary to that right are null and void." WPL lawfully recreated the functionality by observing behaviour, without decompiling. — [Fieldfisher summary](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-limited); [European Law Blog](https://www.europeanlawblog.eu/pub/c-40610-sas-institute-v-world-programming-ltd-wpl)
- Chemaxon itself disclaims ownership of Output (§2.7, quoted in Q1), while reserving rights in "underlying data, algorithms". — [Chemaxon EULA](https://docs.chemaxon.com/latest/legal_end-user-license-agreement-eula.html)
- CC BY 4.0 licenses only rights the licensor holds: "Licensed Rights means the rights granted to You ... which are limited to all Copyright and Similar Rights that apply to Your use of the Licensed Material and that the Licensor has authority to license." — [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)
- CC BY 4.0, "No downstream restrictions": "You may not offer or impose any additional or different terms or conditions on, or apply any Effective Technological Measures to, the Licensed Material if doing so restricts exercise of the Licensed Rights by any recipient". — [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)
- CC FAQ: "Facts are not subject to copyright, nor are the ideas underlying copyrighted content". Also, "use of the facts and ideas embedded within the contents will not require attribution" unless database copyright is implicated. "CC licenses are operative only when applied to material in which a copyright exists". "You should not apply a license to material that you do not own or that you are not authorized to license." All CC licences include a warranty disclaimer. — [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)
- CC 4.0 and sui generis rights: "Section 2(a)(1) grants You the right to extract, reuse, reproduce, and Share all or a substantial portion of the contents of the database". — [CC BY 4.0 legal code §4](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)

### Inferences
- In the US, the Rhea TSV's per-row assertions are facts, so there is no copyright in them for anyone to assert. In the EU, Chemaxon would have no sui generis right in Rhea's database, because Chemaxon did not make the investment in it. SIB may hold one, and SIB has licensed it under CC BY 4.0. Running an algorithm to create data probably does not count as qualifying "obtaining" investment for SIB either (BHB), though Rhea's curation and verification work may count.
- If SIB's Chemaxon licence did not permit redistribution, the remedy would be Chemaxon v SIB in contract. CC BY's warranty disclaimer means downstream users get no assurance. The realistic exposure for a downstream non-licensee is low, because there is no IP right to infringe, but it is not zero. Possibilities include trade-secret or unfair-competition theories, or national law in Hungary or elsewhere. I found no case law applying such theories to software outputs. This is an inference, not a sourced finding.
- Building a tool that reproduces Marvin's behaviour (functionality) is not copyright infringement under SAS v WPL, provided no code, documentation text or protected expression is copied. For a licensee, contract terms can still restrict this, except for the non-overridable observe/study/test right in Software Directive Art. 5(3)/8. That right concerns studying the program's ideas, and whether it extends to republishing outputs or building competitors is untested.
- Ryanair matters if someone argues the Rhea file is an "unprotected database". It would let a contract (e.g. Rhea's terms of use, if any beyond CC BY) restrict use. Rhea's terms are CC BY 4.0, which is permissive, so this cuts in favour of reuse.

### Gaps
- No case law found that specifically addresses copyright or database rights in computed chemical property values or cheminformatics predictions.
- I did not verify Hungarian law on enforcing licence terms against third parties, or unfair-competition doctrines.
- The EU AI Act and the TDM exceptions (DSM Directive Arts 3/4) were not researched in depth. Art. 4 commercial TDM can be opted out of by rightholders, but that presupposes protected works or databases.

## Q3. Documented community practice: redistributing or learning from ChemAxon- or Epik-computed values (EBI, SIB, ChEMBL, RDKit, eQuilibrator, OPERA, pkasolver)

### Takeaway
Major public resources openly redistribute ChemAxon-computed values under permissive or open licences: ChEMBL since release 26 under CC BY-SA 3.0, and Rhea under CC BY 4.0. Several open ML pKa projects either required a vendor licence to regenerate data, or declined to distribute models because of vendor licence terms (pkasolver/Epik). I found no published EBI, SIB or RDKit guidance specifically about learning from ChemAxon outputs.

### Inferences
- Community practice falls into two camps. Some redistribute computed values as data (ChEMBL, Rhea, and eQuilibrator's cached pKa values). Others are cautious about trained models (pkasolver/Epik). The caution concerned the Epik licence held by the trainers, which is consistent with the privity point: the party holding the vendor licence is the one bound.
- ChEMBL's CC BY-SA distribution of CX_* values implies that EMBL-EBI's arrangement with ChemAxon permits public redistribution of those outputs. I found no published statement of that arrangement.
- The new Chemaxon §2.6(b) "create a training dataset" clause reads as a direct response to pkasolver-style practice. It signals the vendor's intent for future licensees.

- No RDKit, EBI or SIB policy statements were found on learning from ChemAxon outputs.

## Q4. Does deriving rules or code from CC BY-NC (IUPAC), CC BY-SA (ChEMBL) or CC BY (Baltruschat) data impose obligations on the rules or code (e.g. CC0 release)?

### Takeaway
CC licences reach only copyright and similar rights, and facts and ideas fall outside them. Rules abstracted from data (for example, "carboxylic acid SMARTS → deprotonated at pH 7.3, pKa ≈ 4–5") are probably not "Adapted Material", so they could be released as CC0. Bundling or redistributing the licensed data itself (lookup tables of IUPAC or ChEMBL values) inside a CC0 package would not be permitted. NC data cannot be relicensed as CC0, SA data must stay under SA, and CC BY data requires attribution.

### Cited Findings
- IUPAC Digitized pKa Dataset: "This material is available for reuse under a CC BY-NC 4.0 license with the following attribution: Reproduced by permission of International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry." Citation: Zheng & Lafontant-Joseph (2026) v2.4a, doi:10.5281/zenodo.7236452. — [IUPAC Dissociation-Constants README](https://github.com/IUPAC/Dissociation-Constants)
- CC NonCommercial means use "primarily intended for or directed toward commercial advantage or monetary compensation". "CC's definition does not turn on the type of user". "CC cannot advise you on what is and is not commercial use." — [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)
- CC licences grant permission only where copyright permission is needed. Uses that don't require permission don't trigger licence conditions. "Facts are not subject to copyright". A modification is an adaptation only when it "manifests sufficient new creativity to be copyrightable". Under 4.0, "certain uses of databases restricted by sui generis database rights also constitute adaptations". — [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)
- CC BY 4.0 "Adapted Material means material subject to Copyright and Similar Rights that is derived from or based upon the Licensed Material and in which the Licensed Material is translated, altered, arranged, transformed, or otherwise modified in a manner requiring permission under the Copyright and Similar Rights held by the Licensor." — [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)
- CC on SA in databases: "CC licenses do not require the collection or the compilation itself to be made available under an SA license". Each SA-licensed work keeps its SA terms. — [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)
- CC on text and data mining: "If you publicly share the results of your mining activity or the data you mined, you should attribute the rights holder." — [Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)
- ChEMBL is licensed CC BY-SA 3.0 Unported. — [ChEMBL LICENSE](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/databases/chembl/ChEMBLdb/latest/LICENSE)
- ML-specific analysis of ShareAlike is unsettled. See the IViR report "Share Alike and ML" (Szkalej & Senftleben, 2024), and the Montreal Data License paper, which proposes ML-specific data licences because CC-SA "creates issues" for ML. — [IViR: Share Alike and ML](https://ivir.nl/publicaties/download/Share-Alike-and-ML.pdf); [Montreal Data License (arXiv 1903.12262)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.12262)
- The Baltruschat/Czodrowski datasets are CC BY 4.0. — [Machine-learning-meets-pKa README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/czodrowskilab/Machine-learning-meets-pKa/master/README.md)

### Inferences
- Hand-written rules (SMARTS patterns plus decisions) informed by consulting NC, SA or BY data are probably facts or ideas, so no CC obligations attach. Even so, citing the sources is good scholarly practice and is low-cost.
- Generated rule tables can contain the data itself (e.g. per-compound pKa values copied from IUPAC). If the extraction is "substantial", EU sui generis rights may attach, and CC 4.0 treats sui generis use as adaptation. Shipping IUPAC-derived values in a CC0 artifact would then breach NC/BY terms, because CC0 permits commercial use and drops attribution. Keep such tables in a separately licensed file (CC BY-NC) or exclude them.
- ChEMBL is CC BY-SA 3.0. The 3.0 unported licence handles sui generis database rights differently from 4.0 (per the CC FAQ, "earlier versions treat them differently"), which adds uncertainty for EU users. Abstracted rules are likely unaffected. Substantial extracts would need to be released under BY-SA.
- Test or benchmark data used only internally for validation, and not redistributed, generally triggers no CC obligations. For NC data, check whether the project is "commercial": CC0 output that others may use commercially is not itself a commercial use by the project. Publishing the NC data alongside it is a separate question.
- Model weights trained on NC or SA data are an unsettled area. Whether weights are "Adapted Material" is contested (IViR). If the open tool is rules-based rather than learned, this risk is smaller.

### Gaps
- No authoritative CC guidance or case law on whether rule sets or model weights derived from CC-licensed data count as adaptations.
- I did not retrieve the exact CC 3.0 Unported treatment of sui generis database rights (whether it waives or licenses them) for ChEMBL.
- IUPAC's underlying permission terms (beyond the CC BY-NC statement) were not reviewed.
